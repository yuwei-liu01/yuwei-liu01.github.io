Multi-Robot Collaborative Assembly

Localization-guided assembly of a timber reciprocal shell

Tongji University · 2024 · Team Workshop Project

Tutors: Xu Zhen, Zhang Ye, Xiang Wang

Our team explored how three robots could assemble a timber reciprocal shell. One robot provided localization support without handling boards; two assembly robots used grippers and cameras to manipulate the timber components and build the reciprocal structure together.

02 / LEARNING EXPLORATION

Trajectory Learning Exploration

The team repeatedly demonstrated and recorded three types of timber overlap action. Each video contains one complete action and forms one trajectory sample; multiple videos of the same action type make up that class’s demonstration data.

Three overlap actions

The target shell uses three different ways of overlapping the boards, shown below. Their order and repetition define the assembly sequence. These structural actions are distinct from the robot’s grip–move–release–pick-next handling cycle.

The grooves on the upper and lower faces have different orientations, so the board’s face and direction must be distinguished in each demonstration.

Markers across time

Eight semicircular marks in different colors were drawn near the grooves to distinguish feature positions and observe motion. A trajectory is the path of the same colored mark across successive video frames—not a line joining the eight marks in a single frame.

The movement of multiple marks within one recording jointly describes the board’s translation and rotation.

Learning exploration and final assembly

The team used these repeated demonstrations to explore machine-learning methods for studying motion paths. Learning performance was limited during the workshop; the final construction primarily relied on localization and coordinated multi-robot operations.

03 / ROBOTIC ASSEMBLY

Localization-Guided Assembly

With the task defined, the team used localization to support robot operations. Environment and component information served different roles in the assembly workflow.

A Locate

Environment localization placed the robotic setup in its workspace, while visual markers helped locate the timber components. The team’s environment-localization approach involved SLAM; the workshop poster describes LiDAR mapping and scan matching, and labels the component markers as AprilTag.

B Coordinate

The team separated localization support from board handling. One robot provided positioning support without carrying timber; the other two robots performed the assembly operations in the shared workspace.

C Manipulate

Each assembly robot used a gripper and camera to handle the boards. After placing a board, the robot released it and picked up the next one, repeating the handling cycle through the assembly sequence.

One board-handling cycle

  1. Grip a board
  2. Move to the target position
  3. Release
  4. Pick the next board

These handling steps execute the three timber overlap actions shown in Trajectory Learning Exploration.
