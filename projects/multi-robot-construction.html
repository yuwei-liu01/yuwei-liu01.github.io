Multi-Robot Collaborative Assembly

Localization-guided assembly of a timber reciprocal shell

Tongji University · 2024 · Team Workshop Project

Our team explored how three robots could assemble a timber reciprocal shell. One robot provided localization support without handling boards; two assembly robots used grippers and cameras to manipulate the timber components and build the reciprocal structure together.

01 / TASK & APPROACH

Task & Approach

The task began with the reciprocal shell: a structure formed by overlapping timber components. We developed the target geometry, identified three overlap actions, and organized their order and repetition into an assembly sequence.

03 / LOCALIZATION-GUIDED ASSEMBLY

Localization-Guided Assembly

With the task defined, the team used localization to support robot operations. Environment and component information served different roles in the assembly workflow.

A Locate

Environment localization placed the robotic setup in its workspace, while visual markers helped locate the timber components. The team’s environment-localization approach involved SLAM; the workshop poster describes LiDAR mapping and scan matching, and labels the component markers as AprilTag.

B Coordinate

The team separated localization support from board handling. One robot provided positioning support without carrying timber; the other two robots performed the assembly operations in the shared workspace.

C Manipulate

Each assembly robot used a gripper and camera to handle the boards. After placing a board, the robot released it and picked up the next one, repeating the handling cycle through the assembly sequence.

One board-handling cycle

  1. Grip a board
  2. Move to the target position
  3. Release
  4. Pick the next board

These handling steps execute the timber overlap actions defined in Task & Approach.
